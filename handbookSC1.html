Drug Protocols
Section 1 · General Information
💊
Medication Administration Policy

All nurses must adhere to the St Vincent's Hospital drug administration policy. Medication administration policies and protocols are located on the intranet within the General Medication Protocols and the Australian Injectable Drug Handbook. Product information is located in MIMS on the intranet.

Key Points

  • Drugs of Addiction/Dependence (DDs) are counted at the end of clinic. Staff rostered at 8am must take out the DD keys from the cupboard. Ensure you are familiar with Enkey.
  • When administering medication ensure: correct patient, drug, dose, route, time, frequency, reason and no allergy.
  • Do not administer any medication if the order is unclear — clarify first with the RMO who prescribed the order.
👥
Medications Requiring Double-Check
Checked by 2 nurses — at least one must be a Grade 2 Year 2+ RN
  • Injectable drugs (IV, subcutaneous, intramuscular)
  • Cytotoxic RN only
  • Oral anticoagulants
  • Pre-operative medication
  • Drugs of Addiction (S8)
  • Drugs of Dependence (S11)
  • Telephone orders
Graduate / EN / Agency StaffMust double check necessary medication with a St Vincent's Grade 2 Year 2+ Registered Nurse or above.
Telephone OrdersTwo nurses (at least one RN Grade 2 Year 2+) must clarify telephone orders for medication. Both must sign the treatment sheet. The Medical Officer must countersign within 24 hours.
Medication ErrorsAn incident report / VHIMS must be completed for any medication error.

Falls Prevention & Management
Section 1 · General Information
⚠️
Falls Risk Management
  • Patients assessed as a high falls risk or a falls 'red alert' require this information to be communicated during clinical handover and documented on the transport/handover sheet
  • High-risk patients should be checked regularly and located in a high-visibility area if possible
  • A staff member should stay with a patient with cognitive impairment who is at risk of falls while in the bathroom
Post-Fall ActionsAll falls must be reported to the Nurse in Charge and Medical Officer. An incident report (VHIMS) must be completed, the incident documented in the patient history, and the NOK must be notified.

Refer to the Falls Prevention and Management Clinical Guidelines for immediate post-fall management instructions.

Bariatric Patients
Section 1 · General Information
🏋️
Bariatric Patient Definition

The term 'Bariatric Patient' is applied to patients unable to be moved using standard lifting, mobility, bed or hygiene equipment due to their size or weight (related to sitting or lying width, not just weight).

Key RequirementInform the ANUM/NUM if you are notified of a patient having bariatric requirements. Ensure that appropriate equipment is available. Contact the Central Equipment Library or Move Smart team.
Bariatric BedsA bariatric bed must be organised beforehand — there are only 3 bariatric beds available in the whole hospital.
👥
Move Smart Considerations
  • A minimum of four (4) staff must be available for all patient transfers
  • Bariatric transport patients via ambulance are to be transferred directly onto a bariatric bed using a hover matt, hoist or three slide sheets and a pat slide
  • Lateral transfers (bed to bed or bed to chair): no less than four (4) staff members
  • Most nursing staff have been educated on how to use the Hover Matt — use for all lateral and side-to-side transfers
  • Attendant-propelled wheelchairs: pushed by two (2) staff members. Encourage patient to assist wherever possible
Policy ReferenceRefer to the Patient Manual Handling — Bariatric Patient Policy on the intranet.

Specialist Clinics provide over 250,000 non-admitted patient appointments per year across 22 different medical and surgical specialties, and receive 85,000 new referrals per annum. Specialist Clinics provide a gateway to the hospital for patients requiring elective surgery and ongoing chronic disease management. Telehealth for Public Clinics
Section 2 · Clinical Area Information
📱
What is Telehealth?

Telehealth is the use of technology to provide health care over both long and short distances — transmitting voice, data, images and information rather than moving care recipients, health professionals or educators. Telehealth consultations are treated as the same quality as face-to-face appointments.

Undertaken with the consent of the patient, with the understanding that the patient can revert to face-to-face appointments if requested.

⚖️
Public vs. Correctional Telehealth

Corrections (Prison) Patients

SVHM has been utilising telehealth for corrections patients since 2016. All correctional facilities have Health Direct Telehealth access.

Must Run On TimeCorrectional telehealth appointments must run on time — processes and staff (guards, nursing, transfer from cell block) are involved. An hour must be left between appointments from the same prison. Must be confirmed with the individual prison facility prior to clinic start.

Public Patients

Platform: Health Direct video call — available on desktop, laptops, tablets and smartphones. Patients navigate to: www.svhm.org.au/telehealth (Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge).

Virtual Waiting RoomPublic patients sit in their virtual waiting room until a staff member answers. The platform shows how long a patient has been waiting. Multiple appointments can be booked back-to-back without issue.

Patient Resources

  • Brochures — located at reception of Red, Blue & 3rd Floor clinics
  • Website — www.svhm.org.au/telehealth

How You Can Help

  • Familiarise yourself with the Healthdirect video call platform
  • Inform NUM of staff requiring platform access (including yourself)
  • Complete the Telehealth competency

Definition

Ultraviolet B (UVB) therapy (phototherapy) is a dermatological treatment using carefully controlled doses of ultraviolet B light to reduce skin inflammation and slow abnormal skin-cell growth. Commonly used for: Psoriasis, Eczema/atopic dermatitis, Vitiligo, Generalised itching, and other inflammatory skin conditions.

Narrowband UVB uses a specific wavelength of approximately 311–312 nanometres. Treatment is delivered through a specialised light cabinet or targeted device, with dose and exposure time prescribed and gradually adjusted according to the patient's skin type, condition and response.

Activity 4: Complex Wound Assessment
Consistent ApproachAssess the whole person → identify wound cause → assess the wound → consider wound edge and surrounding skin → evaluate pain and infection risk → plan review and escalation.
Assessment DomainWhat to ConsiderLearner Notes
Person & HistoryPresenting concern; onset and duration; wound cause; comorbidities; vascular status; diabetes; medications; allergies; smoking; nutrition; continence; mobility; psychosocial impact; goals of care
Wound Location & TypeExact anatomical location; wound type/aetiology; acute or chronic; surgical history; recurrence; pressure injury classification where applicable
MeasurementsLength × width × depth (cm); undermining, tracking or sinus; wound shape; comparison with previous assessment
Wound BedApproximate % and location of viable granulation/epithelial tissue, slough and necrotic tissue
Exudate & OdourAmount, colour, consistency and odour after cleansing; dressing strike-through
Wound Edge & Periwound SkinEdge condition; maceration; erythema; warmth; oedema; induration; excoriation; rash; blistering
Pain & Neurovascular StatusPain score at rest and during care; sensation; distal colour, warmth, movement, pulses, capillary refill
Infection & DeteriorationLocal or systemic signs: increasing pain, spreading erythema, purulent exudate, malodour, delayed healing, fever. Escalate urgently when concerned.
Plan & EvaluationCleansing and dressing plan; pressure redistribution; referrals; patient education; goals; reassessment frequency; escalation plan
Photography & DocumentationObtain consent; follow organisational policy; use approved equipment and storage; include measurement scale if permitted; never use personal device
