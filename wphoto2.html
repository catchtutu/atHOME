Purpose of Wound Photography
Why we take clinical photographs
▼
Wounds Australia 2023 EWMA eHealth
Clinical photographs support: Objective wound assessment, healing trajectory monitoring, interdisciplinary communication, and telehealth review — they supplement but never replace a written assessment.
Six core purposes of clinical wound photography
Key principle: A photograph supplements — it never replaces — written wound assessment. Every image must be interpreted alongside wound measurements, tissue description, exudate, infection assessment, and documented intervention.

The Two-Shot Standard
Every wound episode requires both photographs
▼
Shot 1: anatomical context with surrounding skin · Shot 2: perpendicular close-up with measurement ruler
Shot 1 — Anatomical Overview Captures the wound in context. Shows surrounding skin, anatomical landmarks, and location on the body. Essential for telehealth clinicians who were not present.
Shot 2 — Perpendicular Close-up Camera held directly above and parallel to the wound surface (90° angle). Disposable measurement ruler in frame. Fills the field with wound and immediate periwound skin.
📐
Camera Positioning
Angle, distance & orientation
▼
Perpendicular positioning prevents size distortion and parallax error
Positioning requirements
1
Consistent distance
Maintain the same camera-to-wound distance at every visit to allow accurate serial comparison.
2
Perpendicular angle (90°)
Camera lens held directly above and parallel to the wound surface. Oblique angles introduce parallax and make wounds appear larger or smaller than actual.
3
Consistent orientation
Photograph in the same anatomical orientation at each visit (e.g. wound apex always toward patient's head). Use a note or marker if needed.
4
Consistent framing
Include the same amount of surrounding skin each time to detect periwound changes reliably.
💡
Lighting & Background
Controlling image quality
▼
Even diffuse lighting reveals true tissue colour · harsh flash creates shadows and specular reflections
✔ Do
Diffuse natural or room light
Consistent lighting setup each visit
Neutral background (blue pad or sheet)
✗ Avoid
Harsh direct flash
Shadows across wound bed
Cluttered or colourful backgrounds
Neutral background Place a plain coloured drape or clinical pad adjacent to the wound. This separates the wound visually, prevents skin tone confusion, and removes distracting background elements.
📏
Measurement Scale
Disposable ruler placement
▼
Disposable ruler placed parallel to wound edge · never inside the wound bed
Disposable ruler — best practice Use a single-use paper or plastic measurement ruler in every close-up photograph. Place it along the longest wound axis, adjacent to but not touching the wound edge. Discard after use.
Do not reuse rulers Reusable rulers are a cross-infection risk. Use a new disposable ruler at every visit and photograph before removing it.

Immediately After Photography
Upload, document, delete from device
▼
Capture → Upload to EMR → Document assessment → Delete from device
No images on personal devices Images must not be retained on personal mobile phones or personal cloud services (iCloud, Google Photos, etc.). Clinical photographs belong in the patient's health record.
Post-photography actions
Images uploaded immediately to EMR or approved platform
Images accurately labelled (patient ID, date, wound site)
Written wound assessment completed
Images never replace written documentation
Images deleted from capture device after confirmed upload
Disposable ruler discarded

Written Assessment — Required Companion
Document all components alongside the photograph
▼
EWMA & Wounds Australia position Photographs must be interpreted alongside — never in isolation from — a complete written wound assessment. Each image must accompany documented clinical findings.
Required documentation elements
Wound dimensions (length × width × depth in cm)
Wound bed tissue description
% granulation, slough, necrosis, epithelialisation
Exudate — amount, type and colour
Odour
Wound edge characteristics
Periwound skin condition
Infection assessment findings
Patient symptoms (pain, itch, heat)
Treatment / dressing applied

Telehealth & Remote Review
Photography supporting remote wound assessment
▼
EWMA & Wounds Australia position Standardised photography is the foundation of reliable telehealth wound review. Images from different clinicians and visit dates are only comparable when acquisition technique is consistent.
1
Consistent technique enables serial comparison
A remote clinician reviewing wound progress depends entirely on technical consistency — same angle, distance, lighting, and framing at every visit.
2
Photographs do not replace examination
Even for telehealth, the on-site clinician must complete a full physical assessment. The photo supplements the verbal report.
3
Secure telemedicine platforms only
Images shared for remote review must travel via secure clinical platforms — not SMS, personal email, or social messaging apps.
Emerging direction (Wounds Australia 2023) Mobile EMR applications, AI-assisted wound measurement, and automated image integration into clinical documentation are the direction of travel — toward fully integrated digital wound assessment.
